Transition probability of an N-body system

Transition probabilities are ubiquitous in statistical physics, yet they rarely appear in statistical descriptions of the cosmological structure formation. This is rather surprising because cosmological dynamics pose the kind of questions they are well suited for. Namely, given the initial positions of matter, what is the probability of finding it in a specified configuration later? I noticed this in recent work but did not have the space develop this viewpoint. So, this post is to lay out some of the theory and in doing so show that the statistics of an NNN-body system can be formulated in probabilistic terms.

The key quantity is the transition probability defined as

pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2)≡En0∼p(n0∣q1,q2)[ δD[x1−xt(q1;n0)] δD[x2−xt(q2;n0)] ](1)p_t(\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_2 | \mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2) \equiv\mathbb{E}_{n_0\sim p(n_0|\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)}\big[\,\delta_D[\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_t(\mathbf{q}_1;n_0)]\, \delta_D[\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_t(\mathbf{q}_2;n_0)]\,\big] \tag{1}pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​)≡En0​∼p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)​[δD​[x1​−xt​(q1​;n0​)]δD​[x2​−xt​(q2​;n0​)]](1)

where the measure is given by

p(n0∣q1,q2) Dn0≡p(n0) n0(q1) n0(q2)⟨n0(q1) n0(q2)⟩ Dn0(2)p(n_0\mid\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)\, \mathscr{D} n_0 \equiv p(n_0)\,\frac{n_0(\mathbf{q}_1)\, n_0(\mathbf{q}_2)} {\langle n_0(\mathbf{q}_1)\, n_0(\mathbf{q}_2)\rangle}\,\mathscr{D} n_0 \tag{2}p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)Dn0​≡p(n0​)⟨n0​(q1​)n0​(q2​)⟩n0​(q1​)n0​(q2​)​Dn0​(2)

The aim is to show this is really a conditional measure (as the notation suggests).

Reweighted measure is conditional PDF in the case of point particles

The reweighted pdf has a nice interpretation as a conditional pdf. More precisely, using the discrete distribution for NNN point particles,

n0(q)=∑a=1NδD(q−qa′)n_0(\mathbf{q})=\sum_{a=1}^N\delta_D(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}_a')n0​(q)=a=1∑N​δD​(q−qa′​)

I will show that p(n0∣q1,q2)p(n_0\mid\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)p(n0​∣q1​,q2​) is the probability density about a particle configuration (q1′,…,qN′)(\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N')(q1′​,…,qN′​), given particles at q1\mathbf{q}_1q1​ and q2\mathbf{q}_2q2​.

Suppose we have a configuration of NNN indistinguishable particles, denoted {qa′}={q1′,…,qN′}\{\mathbf{q}_a'\}=\{\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N'\}{qa′​}={q1′​,…,qN′​}. Let

p(n0) Dn0→π(q1′,…,qN′) d3q1′⋯d3qN′ ,p(n_0)\,\mathscr{D} n_0 \to\pi(\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N')\,\mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{q}_1'\cdots\mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{q}_N'\,,p(n0​)Dn0​→π(q1′​,…,qN′​)d3q1′​⋯d3qN′​,

where we use π\piπ instead of ppp to avoid potential confusion with the transition probability ptp_tpt​. (We may think of π\piπ as the prior.) This is the probability of finding particle 1 in the interval (q1′,q1′+dq1′)(\mathbf{q}_1',\mathbf{q}_1'+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_1')(q1′​,q1′​+dq1′​), and particle 2 in the interval (q2′,q2′+dq2′)(\mathbf{q}_2',\mathbf{q}_2'+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q}_2')(q2′​,q2′​+dq2′​), and so on. For short, the probability of obtaining a configuration within a small range about {qa′}\{\mathbf{q}_a'\}{qa′​}. Now fix a particle at q1\mathbf{q}_1q1​ and another at q2\mathbf{q}_2q2​. This is the constraint. The probability density at this configuration, given this constraint, is

p(n0∣q1,q2)≡π(q1′,…,qN′∣q1,q2)=π(q1′,…,qN′,q1,q2)π(q1,q2)=π(q1′=q1,q2′=q2,q3′,…,qN′)+permsπ(q1,q2)(3)\begin{aligned} p(n_0\mid\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2) &\equiv \pi(\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N'\mid\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2) \\[9pt] &= \frac{\pi(\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N',\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)}{\pi(\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)} \\[6pt] &=\frac{\pi(\mathbf{q}_1'=\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2'=\mathbf{q}_2,\mathbf{q}_3',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N')+\text{perms}}{\pi(\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)} \end{aligned} \tag{3}p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)​≡π(q1′​,…,qN′​∣q1​,q2​)=π(q1​,q2​)π(q1′​,…,qN′​,q1​,q2​)​=π(q1​,q2​)π(q1′​=q1​,q2′​=q2​,q3′​,…,qN′​)+perms​​(3)

where the second equality follows by the chain rule. Here π(q1′,…,qN′,q1,q2)\pi(\mathbf{q}_1',\ldots,\mathbf{q}_N',\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2)π(q1′​,…,qN′​,q1​,q2​) should be understood as the intersection of {q1′,…,qN′}\{\q_1',\ldots,\q_N'\}{q1′​,…,qN′​} and {q1,q2}\{\q_1,\q_2\}{q1​,q2​}, the result being all pairs that can be formed from the NNN particles (NNN choose two). To show that Equation 3 reduces to Equation 2, note that the numerator can be written as

π(q1′=q1,q2′=q2,q3′,…,qN′)+perms=2N(N−1)∑a=1N∑b=1NδD(q1−qa′) δD(q2−qb′) π(q1′,…,qN′)\begin{aligned} &\pi(\q_1'=\q_1,\q_2'=\q_2,\q_3',\ldots,\q_N')+\text{perms} \\ &\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad= {\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{a=1}^N\sum_{b=1}^N\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a')\, \delta_D(\q_2-\q_b')\, \pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N')} \end{aligned}​π(q1′​=q1​,q2′​=q2​,q3′​,…,qN′​)+perms=N(N−1)2​a=1∑N​b=1∑N​δD​(q1​−qa′​)δD​(q2​−qb′​)π(q1′​,…,qN′​)​

The combinatorial factor is because there are N(N−1)N(N-1)N(N−1) terms for NNN choose two, and the factor of 2 takes care of the unimportant ordering. Inserting this back into Equation 3, and noting that the sums factorize, we have

π(q1′,…,qN′∣q1,q2)=∑aδD(q1−qa′)∑bδD(q2−qb′) π(q1′,…,qN′)∫q1′⋯∫qN′∑aδD(q1−qa′)∑bδD(q2−qb′) π(q1′,…,qN′)=π(q1′,…,qN′)∑aδD(q1−qa′)∑bδD(q2−qb′)∫q1′⋯∫qN′∑aδD(q1−qa′)∑bδD(q2−qb′) π(q1′,…,qN′)\begin{aligned} \pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N'\mid\q_1,\q_2) &= \frac{\sum_{a}\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a')\sum_{b} \delta_D(\q_2-\q_b')\, \pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N')} {\int_{\q_1'}\cdots\int_{\q_N'} \sum_a\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a') \sum_b\delta_D(\q_2-\q_b')\, \pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N')} \\[12pt] &=\pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N') \frac{\sum_{a}\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a')\sum_{b} \delta_D(\q_2-\q_b')} {\int_{\q_1'}\cdots\int_{\q_N'} \sum_a\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a') \sum_b\delta_D(\q_2-\q_b')\, \pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N')} \\ \end{aligned}π(q1′​,…,qN′​∣q1​,q2​)​=∫q1′​​⋯∫qN′​​∑a​δD​(q1​−qa′​)∑b​δD​(q2​−qb′​)π(q1′​,…,qN′​)∑a​δD​(q1​−qa′​)∑b​δD​(q2​−qb′​)π(q1′​,…,qN′​)​=π(q1′​,…,qN′​)∫q1′​​⋯∫qN′​​∑a​δD​(q1​−qa′​)∑b​δD​(q2​−qb′​)π(q1′​,…,qN′​)∑a​δD​(q1​−qa′​)∑b​δD​(q2​−qb′​)​​

where the denominator (the normalization) is equal to π(q1,q2)\pi(\q_1,\q_2)π(q1​,q2​), up to combinatorial factors, which drop out. Equation 2 is recovered upon identifying that π(q1′,…,qN′)\pi(\q_1',\ldots,\q_N')π(q1′​,…,qN′​) is p(n0)p(n_0)p(n0​) and ∑aδD(q1−qa′)\sum_a\delta_D(\q_1-\q_a')∑a​δD​(q1​−qa′​) is n0(q1)n_0(\q_1)n0​(q1​), so that the denominator is ⟨n0(q1)n0(q2)⟩\langle n_0(\q_1) n_0(\q_2)\rangle⟨n0​(q1​)n0​(q2​)⟩. Starting from the definition of the conditional probability, we have thus shown that the unusual form Equation 2 is recovered.

Example: transition probability for small NNN

To make the previous section more concrete, consider a couple of examples.

For the first example consider a system consisting of N=2N=2N=2 particles in volume VVV. In this case we have a delta function distribution

p(n0∣q1,q2)≡π(q1′,q2′∣q1,q2)=12[δD(q1′−q1) δD(q2′−q2)+(q1′↔q2′)]p(n_0\mid\q_1,\q_2) \equiv \pi(\q_1',\q_2'\mid\q_1,\q_2) =\frac12\big[\delta_D(\q_1'-\q_1)\,\delta_D(\q_2'-\q_2) +(\q_1'\leftrightarrow\q_2')\big]p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)≡π(q1′​,q2′​∣q1​,q2​)=21​[δD​(q1′​−q1​)δD​(q2′​−q2​)+(q1′​↔q2′​)]

where the factor of a half is due to symmetrization of the pair (there is only one distinct configuration possible). This means that knowledge of the two particles being transitioned completely determines the transition probability (Equation 1):

pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2)=∫pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2,n0) p(n0∣q1,q2) Dn0=pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2,n0)∫p(n0∣q1,q2) Dn0=pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2,n0)\begin{aligned} p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2) &=\int p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2,n_0)\, p(n_0\mid\q_1,\q_2)\,\mathscr{D} n_0 \\ &=p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2,n_0) \int p(n_0\mid\q_1,\q_2)\,\mathscr{D} n_0 =p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2,n_0) % \delta_D[\x_1-\x_t(\q_1)]\,\delta_D[\x_2-\x_t(\q_2)]\,, \end{aligned}pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​)​=∫pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​,n0​)p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)Dn0​=pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​,n0​)∫p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)Dn0​=pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​,n0​)​

where in the second line we have used that conditioning on n0n_0n0​ provides no additional information beyond what we already know from conditioning on q1\q_1q1​ and q2\q_2q2​.

In the second example, consider N=3N=3N=3 particles. Here we care about the transitions of only two particles, and are indifferent to the position of the third particle. Thus the transition probability (Equation 1) is given by marginalizing over all possible positions for that particle:

pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2)=∫d3q3′  pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2,q3′) π(q3′∣q1,q2).p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2) =\int\mathrm{d}^3{\q_3'}\; p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2,\q_3')\, \pi(\q_3'\mid\q_1,\q_2).pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​)=∫d3q3′​pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​,q3′​)π(q3′​∣q1​,q2​).

Here we used that p(n0∣q1,q2)≡π(q1′,q2′,q3′∣q1,q2)=π(q3′∣q1,q2) δD(q1−q1′) δD(q2−q2′)p(n_0\mid\q_1,\q_2)\equiv \pi(\q_1',\q_2',\q_3'\mid\q_1,\q_2) =\pi(\q_3'\mid\q_1,\q_2)\,\delta_D(\q_1-\q_1')\,\delta_D(\q_2-\q_2')p(n0​∣q1​,q2​)≡π(q1′​,q2′​,q3′​∣q1​,q2​)=π(q3′​∣q1​,q2​)δD​(q1​−q1′​)δD​(q2​−q2′​) gives the probability of finding a particle in an infinitesimal volume centred at q3′\q_3'q3′​ given particles at q1\q_1q1​ and q2\q_2q2​. (In the second equality we are ignoring the complication to do with the indistinguishability of particles.) Assuming for simplicity that the particles are uniformly random distributed at the initial time, so π(q3′∣q1,q2)=π(q3′)=1/V\pi(\q_3'\mid\q_1,\q_2)=\pi(\q_3')= 1/Vπ(q3′​∣q1​,q2​)=π(q3′​)=1/V, we have

pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2)=1V∫d3q3′  pt(x1,x2∣q1,q2,q3′)p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2) =\frac1V\int\mathrm{d}^3{\q_3'}\; p_t(\x_1,\x_2\mid\q_1,\q_2,\q_3')pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​)=V1​∫d3q3′​pt​(x1​,x2​∣q1​,q2​,q3′​)

In general, if we consider the transition of NNN particles, in a system consisting of precisely NNN particles, then the transition probability pt(x1,…,xN∣q1,…,qN)p_t(\x_1,\ldots,\x_N\mid\q_1,\ldots,\q_N)pt​(x1​,…,xN​∣q1​,…,qN​) is determined. If instead we consider the transition of M<NM<NM<N particles then our ignorance about N−MN-MN−M particles (those not explicitly being transitioned) is marginalized over, yielding a transition probability which is not determined.
